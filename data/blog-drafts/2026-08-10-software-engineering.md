# What Small Business Owners Should Know Before Hiring a Web Builder

Building a website for your cafe, boutique, or dental clinic should feel like opening a second front door for your business. It should bring in new enquiries, show off your products, and make you look professional.

However, many non-technical business owners run into frustrating surprises after hiring a builder. They find themselves locked out of their accounts, forced to rebuild their site from scratch, or hit with unexpected fees for simple updates.

Here are four simple principles every business owner should know before paying anyone to build a website.

## 1. Own Your Keys From Day One

When a carpenter builds a display counter for your shop, they do not keep the keys to your front door. The same rule applies to your website.

Always insist that your primary hosting accounts, website domain name, and service logins are created in your name. If a web agency creates these accounts on your behalf, make sure they transfer full owner access to you immediately. If you ever decide to part ways with a agency, you should still hold complete control of your digital storefront.

## 2. Don't Pay Someone to Reinvent the Wheel

Skilled web builders know how to assemble great sites efficiently. They rely on established, secure tools and ready-made building blocks for common functions like appointment scheduling, contact forms, or photo galleries.

If a builder claims they need months to write custom code for basic features, ask questions. Using proven tools keeps your costs down and makes your site far more reliable.

## 3. Keep Things Clear for the Next Helper

Your business will grow and change over time. You might hire a new assistant next year or work with a different designer in two years. 

Ensure your website builder writes clear notes about how the site was put together. When a site is organized cleanly, any future web professional can pop in, fix a detail, or add a page without having to tear down the entire site and start over.

## 4. Remember That Websites Need Regular Check-ups

Just like your shop's air conditioner or delivery vehicle, a website requires light routine maintenance. Browsers update, safety standards change, and software needs periodic tune-ups to stay fast and secure.

When budgeting for a website, set aside a small amount for monthly or quarterly upkeep so your site never slows down or goes offline unexpectedly.
