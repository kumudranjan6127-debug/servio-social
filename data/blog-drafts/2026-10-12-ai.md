# Common AI Mistakes on Small Business Websites (and Simple Fixes)

Many small business owners are excited about using artificial intelligence on their websites. It sounds wonderful: an assistant that talks to customers while you sleep, writes descriptions for your products, and answers questions instantly.

Used properly, these tools can save you hours of work. But when installed without care, they can easily confuse visitors, slow down your pages, and cost you real enquiries.

Here are five common mistakes business owners make with AI, along with the straightforward fixes to protect your business reputation.

---

### 1. Letting Chatbots Answer Freely

When you give an automated chat tool total freedom to reply, it tries to be helpful by guessing. It might promise a discount you do not offer, give out the wrong business hours, or misunderstand your services entirely.

**The fix:** Lock your chatbot strictly to your own pre-approved list of questions and answers. If a customer asks something outside that list, the bot should politely explain that it does not know and give them an easy button to call or WhatsApp you directly.

### 2. Publishing Raw, Computer-Written Text

AI writing tools can write a full page in seconds. However, that text often sounds hollow, flat, and repetitive. Customers can feel that no human cared enough to write it, which weakens their trust.

**The fix:** Treat text from AI like a rough first draft. Read through it, trim the fluff, and rewrite sentences using your own natural voice. Mention your city, your neighborhood, and how you actually help your customers.

### 3. Adding Heavy Chat Boxes That Freeze Your Page

Many website owners paste in a chat tool only to find their entire site becomes sluggish. On a mobile phone with an average internet connection, visitors end up staring at a blank screen and leaving before your phone number even appears.

**The fix:** Set up the chat box so it only loads after the main parts of your page have finished appearing, or only when a visitor taps the chat bubble.

### 4. Relying on Fake AI Photos

Computer-made photos of smiling models look polished at first glance, but customers quickly notice strange details. It feels artificial and creates doubt about whether your business is genuine.

**The fix:** Use real photos. Pictures of your actual shop counter, your clinic reception, your kitchen, or your team doing real work build instant credibility that no computer graphic can match.

### 5. Collecting Information Without Telling Customers

When visitors type their phone number or personal questions into a chat window, they expect that information to stay private. Some tools quietly use customer conversations to train their systems.

**The fix:** Add a short, simple note explaining that customer details stay private, and check the settings on your tool to make sure your visitors' messages are not saved for public training.
