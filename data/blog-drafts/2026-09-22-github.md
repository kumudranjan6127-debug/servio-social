# How to Undo a Broken Website Update in Minutes (Without Waiting for Your Developer)

It is Friday evening. Your web designer finishes adding a new announcement banner to your salon or clinic website. Everything seems fine, so you both log off for the weekend.

On Saturday morning, a customer sends you a message: your appointment form is gone, and the phone button does nothing when tapped.

You try calling your designer, but their phone is off. In the meantime, dozens of potential clients visit your page, hit a blank wall, and turn to your nearest competitor instead.

This nightmare happens to small business owners all the time. But if your site is set up with GitHub, you can fix the problem yourself in less than five minutes.

## The Automatic Time Machine for Your Site

GitHub sounds like something only programmers use, but at its heart, it is simply a digital safe that keeps track of your website's history.

Think of it like an automatic time machine. Every time your designer changes a photo, adds text, or updates a layout, GitHub saves that change as a distinct snapshot. It never overwrites or destroys the previous working version.

If a new update breaks your checkout counter or contact form, the version from yesterday morning is still sitting there, completely intact.

## How to Press the Emergency Undo Button

You do not need to read code or install complicated tools to restore your site. You can do it straight from a standard web browser on your laptop or phone:

1. Log into your GitHub account.
2. Open your website's folder and click on the **Pull Requests** tab.
3. Find the update that was just added.
4. Click the button labeled **Revert**.

That is all it takes. Modern web hosting services talk directly to GitHub. The moment you confirm that revert, the host automatically reloads the previous working version of your website.

Within minutes, your forms work again, your phone number is clickable, and your business is back on track.

## You Do Not Lose the New Work

A common fear is that undoing a change will erase everything your developer worked on. It doesn't.

Reverting simply tells your live website to display the older, stable snapshot. The broken update stays stored safely in your account. When your developer returns on Monday, they can look at the exact update, find the error, and fix it without your customers ever noticing.

## Always Keep the Master Keys

For this emergency safety net to work, you must hold the administrative account for your website on GitHub. Many freelancers create accounts under their personal emails, leaving owners completely locked out when something breaks.

Always insist that your website files live in an account you own, with your developer added as a team member.
