## Voice

- **Conversational.** Facebook is the most casual of the four platforms — write
  the way you would explain something useful to a business owner across the
  table. Warm, direct, zero jargon.
- Still one idea per post, and still concrete: a real number from the fact sheet
  beats three adjectives.

## Format

- **Length: 40–120 words is the sweet spot.** Long enough for one useful point,
  short enough that nobody scrolls past. Do not pad toward the top of the range.
- Lead with the hook — the fact or the pain point — in the first sentence.
- **1–3 hashtags**, at the end. Hashtags matter less on Facebook than anywhere
  else, so when in doubt use fewer.
- **Include the CTA link in the post text**, with `utm_source=facebook` in the
  tracking suffix. One CTA, one link.
- A blank line between thought and CTA keeps it readable.
- Emojis: one or two are fine here; never rows of them.

## Engagement bait — absolutely none

Meta's algorithm actively demotes posts that fish for interactions. Never use:
"tag a friend", "like if you agree", "comment YES below", "share this with
someone who needs it", or any phrasing whose only job is to farm clicks and
reactions. If the post is useful, people will engage on their own.

## Uniqueness reminder

Meta demotes unoriginal content. This text must be written fresh for Facebook —
different opening, different structure, different emphasis from every other
platform's version of this post.
