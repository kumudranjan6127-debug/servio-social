## The image is mandatory

Instagram cannot publish text alone — every post needs an image. The day's
branded image travels with this caption, so the `image.alt` and `image.headline`
fields in the JSON output matter most here. The headline is the short line
rendered ON the image; the caption you write below should build on it, not
repeat it word for word.

(If the assignment's image plan says "none", flag it in `research_notes` — an
Instagram post cannot run without an image, and the validator will catch it.)

## Format

- **Caption limit: 2,200 characters — but the first 125 characters decide
  everything.** The feed cuts the caption off around 125 characters with a
  "... more" link, so front-load the hook: the sharpest fact or the reader's
  pain point goes in the very first sentence. No warm-up lines.
- After the hook, use **short paragraphs with blank lines between them** so the
  caption is scannable. One idea per paragraph, 1–2 lines each. A mid-length
  caption (300–800 characters) that says something real beats a padded long one.
- **5–10 hashtags, in their own block at the very end** of the caption, after a
  blank line. Mix reach tags with specific ones (#WebsiteDesign alongside
  #SmallBusinessIndia). Never scatter hashtags through the sentences.
- **Links are not clickable in Instagram captions**, so do not paste a long URL
  into the caption. Phrase the single CTA as an action the reader can take —
  pointing to the link in bio, or telling them what to search or send. Still
  exactly one CTA.
- Emojis: a few are at home on Instagram — as visual punctuation at the start of
  a line or to mark a list. Never rows of them.

## Uniqueness reminder

Meta demotes unoriginal content. This caption must be written fresh for
Instagram — different opening, different structure, different emphasis from
every other platform's version of this post.
